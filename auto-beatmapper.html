🎵 Auto Beatmapper

Auto Beatmapper turns any MP3 into a full, playable osu! beatmap — the layout of notes a player taps along to the music. Pick a song, turn a few dials, and get back a ready-to-play map. No rhythm-mapping experience required.

What's a beatmap, anyway?

osu! is a rhythm game: circles appear on screen in time with a song, and the player clicks or taps each one the instant it's "hit." A beatmap is the file that says exactly which circles appear, where on screen, and when — one is hand-built, note by note, for every song you can play. Building one well normally takes a mapper hours of manual placement and playtesting.

This tool skips that: give it an MP3, and it composes the whole layout itself — rhythm, shapes, and on-screen movement — and hands back a ready-to-play .osz map file.

From MP3 to map, in three passes

1. Lay down circles

BPM and beat offset are detected straight from the audio. A circle is then placed on a beat subdivision chosen by how loud and busy that instant sounds — quiet passages get sparse taps, loud ones get dense bursts — so the rhythm tracks the song's own energy instead of a fixed grid.

2. Merge some circles into sliders

Adjacent circles get combined into sliders — holds the player drags along a path, including the occasional back-and-forth "bounce" — breaking up the rhythm instead of leaving every note a single tap.

3. Style it

Every object is repositioned on screen by a set of flow rules — jump distance, curve shape, turn angle, streams vs. stacks — so the map reads as intentionally designed rather than randomly scattered. This pass can be re-run on its own for a different look without touching the rhythm.

Turn the dials

Every generated map is controlled by a handful of parameters — the same song can come out chill or chaotic.

  • Intensity: how much of the song reaches the busiest circle density.
  • Slider mix & length: how often taps get merged into sliders, and how long those holds run.
  • Jump distance: how far apart objects land on screen.
  • Creativity: how tightly the styling sticks to a pattern vs. varies section to section.
  • Streams & stacks: how often fast runs become deliberate streams, piled or spread.
  • Difficulty tiers: Insane is always built; Hard/Normal/Easy are thinned and re-styled, not just shrunk copies.

Does it actually feel like a real map?

An optional report compares the generated difficulties — or a hand-mapped beatmap — side by side, then grades each one against osu!'s own ranking criteria: delay between objects, on-screen jump spacing, slider length and duration, and the mix of turn angles across the map.

Every difficulty gets a pass / warn / fail verdict per check — AR/OD/HP/CS ranges, off-screen objects, illegal overlaps, slider velocity, stream length, and more — so a bad generation is caught before it ever reaches a player.

Under the hood

Every run is seeded and reproducible, so the same song and the same dials always produce the same map.
