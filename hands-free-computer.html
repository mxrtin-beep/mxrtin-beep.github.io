Computers used to be used without a mouse. Then, Xerox created a little tool you can use to navigate a computer. Then, Apple took the next step with FingerWorks, and created the Trackpad and iPhone, which allowed you to fluidly glide your finger over smooth glass and metal.

The next logical step is getting rid of the metal and glass entirely. What is smoother than air?

This project will allow you to control a computer with nothing other than your webcame and hand. It uses the OpenCV (cv2) package to utilize your webcam and capture each frame. Then, Google's mediapipe locates 20 points on your hand; we use just the fingertips. Finally, the program uses pyautogui to give commands to your mouse and keyboard.

Current Progress

The project, now called FingerWorks, has grown from a proof of concept into a full hand-tracking control system with a packaged Windows build (latest release) that runs with no Python install needed.

Gestures

  • ✋ Move your right hand → moves the cursor (follows the middle finger)
  • 🤏 Pinch right thumb + index → left click (hold to drag)
  • 🤏 Pinch right thumb + ring → right click
  • ✊ Close your right hand into a fist → toggle the on-screen keyboard
  • 🤙 "Hang Ten" sign with your right hand → pause tracking
  • ✋ Open left palm → zoom in, ✊ left fist → zoom out
  • ☝️ Left index finger up → scroll up, 👎 left thumb down → scroll down

Features

  • A small always-on-screen control bar (Pause, Help, Settings, Quit) so you never need a gesture or the command line just to pause or quit
  • A full on-screen keyboard laid out like a phone keyboard, including Shift/Caps, Undo/Redo, Select All, clipboard keys, and a symbols page
  • A Settings window for camera selection, debug mode, and feel-tuning sliders: mouse sensitivity, cursor snappiness, scroll speed, and keyboard size, plus optional click/keystroke sound feedback
  • Two independent smoothing layers between your fingertip and the cursor, so small precise movements and large fast ones can each be tuned separately
  • Debug mode with a live camera feed, hand-tracking overlay, and diagnostic text for troubleshooting gestures
  • Per-frame error handling so a camera or tracking hiccup no longer crashes the whole program
