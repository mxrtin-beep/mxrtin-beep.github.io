🛰️ Besseleth

Besseleth is a bot that watches an entire industry for you. Tell it what field you care about — I built it for neurotech — and it keeps a running, permanent record of the papers, news, blog posts, conferences, funding rounds, job postings, and events happening in it, then hands you a digest instead of forty open tabs.

The idea started from a dumb problem: staying current in a niche field means checking a dozen different sources by hand, and most of them either bury the signal in noise (news feeds) or bury it in jargon (papers). Besseleth just does the checking for you, on a schedule, and remembers everything it's ever seen so nothing has to be re-read twice.

How it pulls things in

What makes it more than a news feed

It reads for meaning, not just keywords

An LLM pass tags every item with what org it's about, what technique it uses, what it's trying to treat, and how novel it actually is compared to everything besseleth has already logged — so a paper claiming a "breakthrough" information transfer rate gets checked against the actual best number on record before it's allowed to sound impressive.

It knows who you know

Add your contacts (name, company, school) and any item that touches one of their companies — a job posting, a funding round, a paper out of their old lab — gets pulled into its own "for you" section instead of getting lost in the general feed.

It tracks the field over time, not just this week

Devices, companies, stock prices, and org-level milestones (funding, FDA clearances, acquisitions) all accumulate in the same database forever, so you can chart something like implant electrode count or a competitor's stock history release over release, not just read about the latest one.

The dashboard

A browser extension rounds it out: highlight anything on LinkedIn, X, or Bluesky and a little "+ besseleth" button clips it straight into the paste box, since none of those platforms hand out a free way to search them.

Under the hood

Python, SQLite, and Flask for the dashboard, with Plotly for the charts. Summarization runs on Groq's free tier by default and falls back to a local Ollama model if you'd rather keep everything on your own machine. Everything else — arXiv, OpenAlex, OpenStreetMap geocoding, RSS — is free and keyless, so the whole thing can run indefinitely for nothing.
